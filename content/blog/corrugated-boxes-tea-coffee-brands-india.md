Tea and coffee are among the most packaging-sensitive product categories in food retail. The product inside, whether it is a tin of Darjeeling first flush, a bag of single-origin espresso, or a retail display of herbal sachets, faces specific hazards during transit that generic corrugated solutions do not account for.

Corrugated boxes for tea and coffee brands serve two distinct supply chain functions, and the specification requirements differ significantly between them.

**Retail wholesale** involves shipping cases of product to retail buyers: supermarkets, independent food stores, garden centres, delis. These boxes carry a dozen tins, a hundred sachets, or six bags of ground coffee. They are opened in a stockroom and discarded. The priority is crush resistance, consistent dimensions, and reliable print for barcode and lot number compliance.

**Direct-to-consumer (DTC)** involves the box arriving in a customer's hands as part of the brand experience. Subscription tea boxes, sample packs, and gifting formats depend on the unboxing to reinforce brand values. Custom print, premium finishes, and tight dimensional tolerances matter here in a way they do not for wholesale cases.

Most Indian corrugated manufacturers can supply both formats. The question is specifying correctly for each.

## Flute Selection for Tea and Coffee Products

Getting the flute grade right is the most common mistake buyers make on a first order. The specification depends on what goes inside the box. For a detailed technical comparison of B, C, and E flute profiles, see our [corrugated flute guide for buyers](/blogs/b-flute-vs-c-flute-vs-e-flute-corrugated-boxes).

**E flute (1.2mm)** is the standard for retail-facing corrugated. It gives a smooth outer surface for high-quality flexographic print, takes colour well, and is lightweight. Most branded tea tins, glass coffee jars, and premium retail pouches are shipped in E flute cartons.

**B flute (3mm)** is used where stacking strength matters more than print surface. Wholesale master cartons going to a distributor or retailer back-of-house often use B flute. Coffee bags in flexible film packaging, which are not fragile but need a firm outer wall, suit B flute well.

**C flute (4mm)** handles heavy product stacking. Multiple glass coffee jars in a single case, or large-format tea tins, call for C flute or a BC double-wall laminate. For a full breakdown of single-wall versus double-wall configurations, see our [single vs. double wall corrugated guide](/blogs/single-wall-vs-double-wall-corrugated-boxes).

| Product Type | Recommended Flute | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Retail tea tins, glass jars | E flute | Print quality, shelf-ready appearance |
| Flexible coffee pouches, sachets | B flute | Compression resistance for stacking |
| Heavy glass jars, large-format tins | C flute or BC double | Stacking load and fragility protection |
| Premium subscription boxes (DTC) | E flute or EB double | Print quality combined with rigidity |

## Custom Printing for Tea and Coffee Brand Packaging

Tea and coffee brands invest heavily in label design and brand identity. The corrugated box is often treated as an afterthought, but for DTC brands and premium wholesale accounts, it is another brand surface.

Indian corrugated manufacturers supply two main print processes:

**Flexographic printing** is the standard for corrugated. It deposits ink directly onto the board using flexible printing plates. Up to four colour separation is available from most factories. The result is accurate enough for brand colours, product photography, and clean typography, but it will not match the sharpness of lithographic print.

**Litho laminate** applies offset-printed paper to the outer liner before box conversion. The result is photo-quality print on corrugated: sharp images, smooth gradients, and the option for embossed or foil elements. Premium tea brands using their boxes as retail display or gifting elements typically specify litho laminate for their outer cartons.

## Certifications That Matter for Tea and Coffee Packaging

Food brands face tighter certification requirements than general retail. Corrugated boxes for tea and coffee typically need to meet:

**Food contact certification** - The liner papers used in corrugated board must be certified safe for incidental food contact. In practice, the inner liner of the corrugated box does not touch the tea or coffee directly, as the product sits in its primary packaging, but buyers for large grocery chains often require food-contact board certification regardless.

**FSC certification** - The Forest Stewardship Council certification confirms the wood pulp in the corrugated board comes from responsibly managed forests. UK and European grocery buyers increasingly require FSC board as a condition of listing. Most large Indian corrugated manufacturers hold FSC chain-of-custody certification.

**Recycled content declaration** - Eco-conscious tea and coffee brands often want corrugated boxes with a specified minimum recycled content. Indian factories producing for export typically use Kraft liner with 70-100% recycled fibre content and can provide a material declaration for sustainability reporting requirements.

**ISO 9001** - Quality management certification. A baseline requirement for buyers who need consistent grammage, print registration, and dimensional tolerances across repeat orders.

## What to Ask Your Indian Corrugated Manufacturer

Before confirming a production run for tea or coffee packaging, ask these questions:

1. **What liner grades do you use?** Request the grammage specification for both outer and inner liners, and the flute medium. A factory using lightweight recycled liner may meet the visual specification but fail on compression.

2. **Do you hold FSC chain-of-custody certification?** Ask for the certificate number and verify it directly at info.fsc.org.

3. **What is your dimensional tolerance?** For boxes that need to fit into retail shelf fixtures or standard pallet configurations, dimensional accuracy within plus or minus 2mm is the benchmark.

4. **Can you supply a compression test report?** Box Compression Test (BCT) values confirm stacking strength. The factory should be able to provide BCT data for your specified flute grade and box dimensions.

5. **What print registration tolerance do you work to?** For E flute cartons with brand colours and detailed artwork, a registration tolerance of 1.5mm or better is the industry standard.

## Lead Times and MOQ for Tea and Coffee Brands

Standard production lead times from an Indian corrugated manufacturer to UK or US port run 35 to 45 days from approved artwork and confirmed order. This includes:

- 7 to 10 days for plate making and pre-production approval
- 10 to 15 days for the production run
- 20 to 28 days sea freight transit

The minimum order quantity for custom corrugated boxes from India varies by factory and box complexity. E flute custom print cartons typically require a minimum of 5,000 units per SKU. B or C flute wholesale cases can often run from 2,000 units.

For brands with multiple SKUs, different tea varieties or coffee origin lines each needing their own box, Indian factories will sometimes combine SKUs in a single production run to help buyers reach minimum quantities more efficiently across the full range.

## Why Source Corrugated Boxes from India

India has over 1,400 corrugated box manufacturers, concentrated in industrial clusters around Rajkot, Ahmedabad, Pune, Delhi-NCR, and Chennai. The sector has modernised significantly over the past decade and now serves food and beverage brand buyers in the UK, US, EU, and Australia directly.

For tea and coffee brands, three factors make India the right sourcing decision:

**Cost advantage** - Factory-direct pricing without European or US converter mark-up results in a lower landed cost per unit at scale, even after sea freight.

**Vertical integration** - Many Indian factories produce their own corrugated board from jumbo rolls in-house rather than purchasing pre-made sheets. This gives tighter control over board specification and a faster response when reorders are needed.

**Export infrastructure** - Major Indian corrugated exporters have established logistics relationships with freight forwarders on the India-UK and India-US lanes. Buyers can receive door-to-door FCL pricing without managing freight themselves.
