When you source corrugated boxes from India, the specification sheet your supplier sends will list a strength rating. It might read **32 ECT**, or it might say **200# Mullen Burst**, or it might show both. If you are a UK or US buyer unfamiliar with the other system, it is easy to assume the rating is wrong - or worse, to order the wrong board grade entirely.

This guide explains both strength standards, why they exist, how Indian manufacturers work with them, and how to specify exactly what you need before your order goes into production. ## What is the Mullen Burst Test?

The Mullen Burst Test (also called the "pop test" or Mullen Test) measures how much hydraulic pressure a sheet of corrugated board can withstand before it ruptures. A circular rubber diaphragm pushes upward against the board until it bursts. The result is measured in pounds per square inch (psi) in the US, or kilopascals (kPa) in metric markets.

A box rated at **200 Mullen** can withstand 200 psi of direct pressure before the board ruptures.

The test was developed in 1887 by John W. Mullen and was the dominant US standard through most of the 20th century. Historically, UK buyers used similar burst-based specifications. Because the test simulates puncture resistance - how well a box resists a sharp, concentrated impact - it was considered a useful proxy for shipping durability in an era of manual handling and direct stacking by hand.

One practical limitation of the Mullen test is that it tests the board itself, not the finished box. A box with a high Mullen rating can still fail in transit if the flute direction, box design, or closure method is wrong. That is why the industry gradually shifted to performance-based testing.

## What is the ECT Rating (Edge Crush Test)?

The Edge Crush Test (ECT) measures the stacking strength of corrugated board. A small sample of board is stood on its edge and compressed vertically until it fails. The result is measured in pounds per lineal inch (lb/in) in the US, or kilonewtons per metre (kN/m) in metric markets.

A box rated **32 ECT** can withstand 32 pounds of force per lineal inch along its top edge before it collapses.

![Close-up view of stacked corrugated board edges showing the layered structure that gives boxes their edge compression strength](https://images.pexels.com/photos/17260158/pexels-photo-17260158.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

ECT was developed to better reflect real-world shipping conditions. In modern supply chains, boxes spend most of their life stacked: in trucks, in shipping containers, on warehouse racking, and in fulfilment centre shelving. The relevant failure mode is not puncture resistance - it is the bottom box in a pallet stack being crushed by the weight above it. ECT directly measures this failure mode.

The International Safe Transit Association (ISTA) and most major US retailers including Amazon now specify boxes by ECT rather than Mullen. The test method is standardised under TAPPI T 811 (US) and ISO 3037 (international).

## Why the US Switched from Mullen to ECT

The shift happened gradually from the 1990s onwards, driven by three structural changes in how goods are moved and stored.

**Palletisation changed how boxes fail.** As warehouses moved to pallet racking and automated conveying systems, stacking compression became the primary stress a corrugated box faces. A Mullen-rated box could pass the burst test convincingly but still collapse under pallet load after a few days in a warehouse. ECT predicted real-world stack performance far more accurately.

**Lighter-weight boards became viable.** Improvements in high-performance fluting grades and liner paper quality allowed manufacturers to produce boards with equal or better stacking strength at lower basis weight (grammage). ECT rewarded this efficiency directly - a lighter board can score as well as a heavier one if its fibre arrangement is optimised. Mullen penalised lighter boards regardless of their actual performance in transit.

**Retailers mandated it.** Amazon's fulfilment network, Walmart supplier standards, Target, Home Depot, and most major US retailers now reference ECT in their packaging specifications, often alongside ISTA or ASTM test protocols. Any UK or European brand selling into the US market through FBA, wholesale, or DTC needs ECT-rated boxes. ## How UK Buyers Specify Box Strength

The UK and European market historically used Mullen Burst or the related Concora Medium Test (CMT) for fluting grade specification. The landscape has shifted considerably since the mid-2000s, and most UK buyers now encounter a mix of standards depending on their supply chain.

**Current UK and European practice:**

- **ECT (kN/m)** is the standard for most UK and EU corrugated board under BS EN ISO 3037. This is increasingly what UK retailers, logistics providers, and export packing specifications reference.
- **BCT (Box Compression Test)** measures the finished box rather than the raw board. BCT in kilonewtons (kN) is growing in use for retail and export compliance because it accounts for box design, closure quality, and moisture content - factors that board-level ECT does not capture.
- **Burst strength (kPa)** is still quoted by some older UK mills and regional converters, particularly for heavier board grades used in industrial or returnable packaging applications.

For UK buyers importing from India, the most practical approach is to specify the board composition directly (liner gsm and grade, fluting gsm and grade, number of plies, flute type) alongside a minimum ECT or BCT value. This removes ambiguity regardless of which internal testing standard your Indian supplier uses.

## How Indian Manufacturers Handle Both Standards

India's corrugated box industry ranges from small regional converters to large integrated paper and board mills with full in-house testing laboratories. Most established exporters supplying UK and US buyers are familiar with both ECT and Mullen ratings, but their internal quality control systems typically prioritise one or the other depending on their customer mix.

**What to expect from Indian suppliers:**

- Exporters with a US retail or Amazon FBA customer base will test to ECT (TAPPI T 811) and can provide ECT test certificates. Ask specifically for test certificates rather than a supplier-declared rating.
- Exporters with European or UK customer bases may still report Mullen Burst or BCT as their primary metric. Most can test to either standard on request.
- High-quality Indian corrugated manufacturers use kraft liner and semi-chemical fluting from certified paper mills, producing consistent ECT performance across production runs. Recycled liner options are also available for buyers prioritising sustainability - recycled and FSC-certified board grades meet the same ECT specifications as virgin kraft at equivalent grammage.

**Common board grades and their approximate strength equivalents:**

| Construction | Typical ECT (lb/in) | Typical Mullen (psi) | Common Application |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3-ply single wall, light | 23-29 ECT | 125-150# | Light ecommerce, apparel, soft goods |
| 3-ply single wall, standard | 32-40 ECT | 175-200# | General export, retail cartons |
| 3-ply single wall, heavy | 44 ECT | 200-225# | Heavier consumer goods |
| 5-ply double wall | 44-61 ECT | 200-275# | Bulky or heavy goods, industrial |
| 7-ply triple wall | 71+ ECT | 350#+ | Very heavy, fragile, or dense goods |

## Which Standard Should You Specify?

**If you are a US buyer:**

Specify ECT. Use **32 ECT** as the baseline for standard ecommerce, retail, and D2C boxes. Step up to **44 ECT** for heavier items, multi-box pallet shipments, or anything going into a club store format where stacking height is significant. For Amazon FBA: 32 ECT with B-flute or C-flute is the standard minimum. Some prep centres and Amazon itself may ask for an ISTA 2A or ISTA 3A test report - request this from your supplier before shipment if your product category requires it.

**If you are a UK buyer:**

Specify ECT in kN/m (divide the lb/in value by 5.71 to convert, e.g. 32 lb/in = 5.6 kN/m) or request a board composition with a BCT minimum. For retail-ready packaging going into UK grocery or general merchandise supply chains, a BCT minimum of 3kN for a standard export carton is a common starting point. For export-grade industrial packaging where Mullen Burst is still referenced in your supplier agreements, a minimum of 300 kPa (approximately 43 psi) is a reasonable baseline for medium-duty applications.

**If you are unsure which grade you need:**

Provide your gross product weight per box and your packed box dimensions to your supplier. A reputable Indian manufacturer will back-calculate the required board grade using McKee's compression formula or their own compression modelling, accounting for pallet configuration and anticipated storage duration. Request the calculation in writing alongside the test certificate.

## Environmental Credentials and Sustainable Options

Higher ECT performance does not require virgin fibre. Many of the corrugated grades supplied by Indian manufacturers use a blend of recycled and virgin kraft liner, achieving 32-44 ECT with post-consumer recycled content. FSC-certified board grades are available for buyers supplying UK and EU markets where retailer sustainability requirements specify chain-of-custody certification.

Water-based printing inks and soy-based coatings are standard at quality Indian exporters, keeping the finished box fully recyclable at end of life. For buyers with B Corp commitments or scope 3 emissions targets, packaging sourced from India at lower cost can free budget for offsetting programmes - a common strategy among UK sustainable consumer brands.

## Key Takeaways

- **Mullen Burst** measures puncture resistance in psi or kPa. It was the dominant standard until the 1990s and remains in use in some UK industrial and older supply chain specifications.
- **ECT** measures stacking strength in lb/in or kN/m. It is the primary standard for US retail, Amazon FBA, and most modern UK and EU corrugated packaging specifications.
- **Indian manufacturers** can supply to either standard. Always request a test certificate specifying the exact test method (TAPPI T 811 for ECT, TAPPI T 810 for Mullen Burst) rather than accepting a declared rating alone.
- For US buyers: **32 ECT minimum** for standard boxes. For UK buyers: specify ECT in kN/m or board composition with a BCT minimum in kN.
- Recycled content and FSC-certified board grades are available at the same ECT ratings as virgin kraft - sustainable packaging does not require a trade-off on structural performance.
