One of the most common questions from buyers new to sourcing packaging from India is: "How long does this actually take?" The answer involves several sequential stages  -  supplier evaluation, sampling, production, sea freight, and customs  -  each with its own timeline. Getting this wrong leads to stock-outs, rushed air freight, or delayed product launches.

This guide gives you a realistic, stage-by-stage breakdown of the full timeline from first enquiry to goods in your warehouse, covering both corrugated boxes and cotton seed bags from India.

## The Full Timeline: From First Enquiry to Warehouse

For a first-time order from a new Indian packaging supplier, allow **10 to 14 weeks** in total from your first enquiry to goods arriving at your warehouse. This is not manufacturer slowness  -  it is the sum of several necessary sequential steps, most of which have fixed minimum durations regardless of supplier quality.

For repeat orders from an established supplier with an approved specification, the timeline compresses significantly to **7 to 9 weeks** because sampling and approval stages are already complete.

Here is each stage:

---

## Stage 1: Supplier Enquiry and Quotation (1–2 Weeks)

**What happens:** You contact potential suppliers with your packaging specification. They review the requirement, price it, and respond with a quotation.

**Realistic timeline:** 3 to 7 business days per supplier for a detailed quotation on a standard specification. Custom requirements with unusual materials or certifications may take up to 2 weeks.

**What to provide to get a fast, accurate quote:**
- Product dimensions (L × W × H in mm for boxes; size in cm for bags)
- Quantity required
- Construction specification (flute type and board grade for boxes; fabric weight and weave for bags)
- Print requirements (colours, logo, text)
- Delivery destination and Incoterms preference (FOB or CIF)

The more specific your specification, the faster and more accurate the quotation. Vague enquiries ("I need corrugated boxes, what's your price?") generate vague responses and multiple rounds of clarification.

---

## Stage 2: Sampling (2–3 Weeks)

**What happens:** Before committing to bulk production, most buyers request pre-production samples. The manufacturer produces a small quantity (typically 20 to 100 units) to the specified dimensions, construction, and print requirements, then ships them for your approval.


![Workers at an Indian packaging manufacturing facility producing goods for export to USA and UK](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1605732562742-3023a888e56e?w=1200&q=80&auto=format&fit=crop)
**Realistic timeline:**
- Sample production: 7 to 14 days after specification is confirmed
- Sample shipment to USA/UK: 5 to 10 days by courier (DHL, FedEx)
- Your review and approval: depends on your internal process

Total sampling stage: **2 to 4 weeks** including transit and approval.

**Can you skip sampling?** For repeat orders of a previously approved product, yes. For first-time orders, skipping sampling is a meaningful risk  -  it removes your only opportunity to catch specification errors before 10,000 units are produced. The cost of a sample run (typically $150–$400 USD for courier shipping, with sample production often free or at nominal cost) is trivially small compared to the cost of a non-conforming bulk production.

**Pro tip:** Provide written approval of samples in email and keep the sample units. These become your quality reference standard during production inspection.

---

## Stage 3: Production (3–5 Weeks)

**What happens:** After sample approval, you issue a purchase order and the manufacturer begins bulk production.

**Realistic production timelines:**

| Product | Standard Timeline | Variables That Extend It |
|---------|------------------|--------------------------|
| Corrugated boxes, single-wall, no print | 18–22 days | Custom sizes, heavy board grades |
| Corrugated boxes, single-wall, printed | 22–28 days | Multi-colour printing, white liner |
| Corrugated boxes, double/triple-wall | 25–35 days | Board weight, custom construction |
| Cotton seed bags, plain/unprinted | 20–25 days | Custom sizes, canvas weight |
| Cotton seed bags, custom printed | 25–35 days | Certification requirements (GOTS) |
| Mixed order (boxes + bags) | 28–35 days | Dependent on longer-lead item |

**What affects production time most:**
- **Board/fabric availability:** If the specific paper grade or cotton fabric weight is not in the manufacturer's current stock, they order from their paper mill or fabric supplier first. This is common for non-standard specifications and adds 5 to 10 days.
- **Print complexity:** Multi-colour flexographic printing requires plate preparation and press setup. One-colour printing is significantly faster than four-colour.
- **Order size:** Very large orders (100,000+ units) may require multiple production runs and extend the timeline.
- **Factory load:** January–March and September–October are peak production periods for Indian manufacturers. Allow additional time for orders placed during these windows.

---

## Stage 4: Pre-Shipment Inspection (Optional, 2–5 Days)

**What happens:** A third-party quality inspector visits the factory before goods are loaded, checks production against your specification, and issues an inspection report.

**Should you do this?** For first and second orders from a new supplier, pre-shipment inspection is strongly recommended. The cost is typically $250–$500 USD for a one-day factory visit by a local inspection company (Intertek, Bureau Veritas, SGS, or local equivalents). The inspection report documents any non-conformances before goods leave India  -  at which point remediation is relatively easy. After goods are loaded on a ship, options are extremely limited.

For established suppliers with multiple completed orders and a quality track record, pre-shipment inspection becomes optional.

**Timeline impact:** Pre-shipment inspection adds 2 to 5 days to the schedule (to arrange the inspector and receive the report), but does not delay shipment if arranged in advance.

---

## Stage 5: Export Documentation and Port Handling (1–2 Weeks)

**What happens:** After production is complete and inspected, the manufacturer arranges export packing, palletisation, and documentation. The goods are transported from the factory to the port, cleared through Indian export customs, and loaded onto the vessel.


![Cargo containers stacked at an Indian export port ready for sea freight departure](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1605732562084-f528a2154616?w=1200&q=80&auto=format&fit=crop)
**Key documents for USA and UK imports:**
- Commercial invoice
- Packing list
- Certificate of origin (preferably from an Indian Chamber of Commerce for tariff preference)
- Bill of lading (sea freight) or airway bill (air freight)
- Phytosanitary certificate (if required for natural fibre products  -  required for cotton bags entering the USA)
- GSP certificate (if applicable for duty reduction  -  check current status for India–UK trade)

**Realistic timeline:** 7 to 10 days from production completion to vessel departure. This includes factory to port transport, customs clearance, and port handling. If goods miss a vessel departure (ships sail on fixed schedules, typically weekly from major Indian ports), add 7 days for the next sailing.

**Major export ports from India for packaging:**
- **JNPT (Mumbai/Nhava Sheva):** Largest container port in India. Weekly departures to USA and UK from multiple carriers.
- **Mundra (Gujarat):** Second largest container port. Strong for shipments from Gujarat and Rajasthan manufacturers.
- **Chennai:** Southeast India. Better for manufacturers in Tamil Nadu.

---

## Stage 6: Sea Freight Transit (18–28 Days)

**What happens:** Your goods are in a container on a vessel crossing the Indian Ocean and either the Suez Canal (for UK/Europe) or the Pacific (for USA West Coast).

**Realistic transit times:**

| Origin Port | Destination | Typical Transit Time |
|-------------|-------------|---------------------|
| JNPT / Mundra | UK (Felixstowe/Southampton) | 20–26 days |
| JNPT / Mundra | USA East Coast (New York/Savannah) | 21–28 days |
| JNPT / Mundra | USA West Coast (Los Angeles) | 18–22 days |
| Chennai | UK | 22–28 days |

These are typical direct or one-transshipment routings. Services with multiple transshipments (changing ships at intermediate ports) are cheaper but add 5 to 10 days and increase handling risk. For packaging (a cost-sensitive commodity), direct or single-transshipment routings are the recommended standard.

**Current disruptions:** Sea freight schedules are subject to periodic disruption from port congestion, weather events, and geopolitical factors affecting specific routes. At booking, your freight forwarder can advise on current routing reliability.

---

## Stage 7: Destination Port Clearance and Delivery (1–2 Weeks)

**What happens:** Your shipment arrives at the destination port, is customs-cleared, and delivered to your warehouse.

**USA import process:**
- ISF (Importer Security Filing) must be filed 24 hours before vessel departure from India  -  your customs broker handles this
- FDA prior notice (if required for natural fibre products)
- CBP customs clearance on arrival: 1 to 5 business days for standard packaging goods
- Drayage from port to warehouse: 1 to 5 days depending on warehouse proximity to port

**UK import process:**
- CHIEF or CDS customs declaration on arrival
- Standard clearance: 1 to 3 business days for packaging goods
- Delivery from port to warehouse: 1 to 3 days

For UK-specific detail on port options, customs duty rates, and EPR compliance documentation for both corrugated boxes and cotton bags, see our [corrugated boxes UK import guide](/blogs/corrugated-boxes-uk-import-guide) and [cotton seed bags UK buyers guide](/blogs/cotton-seed-bags-uk-buyers-guide).

**Total destination handling:** 7 to 14 days from vessel arrival to goods in warehouse.

## How to Plan Your Orders

Given these lead times, the key to avoiding stock-outs is planning orders before you run out of current stock  -  not when you run out.

**For first-time orders:** Start the supplier evaluation and sampling process 16 weeks before you need goods. This gives buffer for sampling revisions, production delays, and customs clearance.

**For repeat orders:** Place orders with 10 to 12 weeks of current stock remaining. This keeps you above zero with buffer even if a vessel is delayed.

**For seasonal peaks:** Indian manufacturers experience higher demand in September–October (pre-Christmas) and January–February (post-Chinese New Year alternative sourcing). Place orders for seasonal peaks 2 to 4 weeks earlier than your standard calculation would suggest.

---

## Air Freight: When It Makes Sense

Sea freight is the standard for bulk packaging orders. Air freight from India to the USA/UK takes 3 to 5 days but costs 6 to 10× more per kg than sea freight. For corrugated boxes and cotton bags, air freight is almost never economical for bulk orders.

The only scenarios where air freight is justified:
- Emergency stock-out where sea freight cannot arrive in time
- Very small first samples (though courier DHL/FedEx is more practical than air freight for samples)
- High-value packaging components where freight cost is a small percentage of product value

For the vast majority of packaging sourcing from India, sea freight via FCL (Full Container Load) or LCL (Less than Container Load) is the correct mode.
