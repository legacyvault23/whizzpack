India has thousands of corrugated box manufacturers. The industry is concentrated in a handful of industrial clusters - Rajkot, Ahmedabad, and Surat in Gujarat; Pune and Nashik in Maharashtra; Delhi NCR; and Chennai - but manufacturers operate in almost every major city. For a US or UK importer looking to source factory-direct, the challenge is not finding suppliers. It is distinguishing the capable, reliable manufacturers from the brokers, the undercapitalised operations, and the factories that will underperform on quality once the first order is placed.

This guide gives you the framework to evaluate corrugated box manufacturers in India before committing to an order.

## Start With the Right Search Channels

**Trade directories:** IndiaMART and TradeIndia list thousands of corrugated box manufacturers. Most listings are self-submitted and unverified. They are a useful starting point for finding contact details, but they are not a quality filter. Use them to build a longlist, not a shortlist.

**Export data services:** Platforms like Volza, ImportGenius, and Panjiva track Indian export shipment records (HS code 4819 for corrugated boxes). You can search for manufacturers who have verifiably exported to the US or UK and see the names of their buyers. Manufacturers with an established export history are lower-risk than those with none.

**Trade fairs:** India ITME, PackEx India, and international trade fairs like FACHPACK (Germany) are attended by Indian packaging manufacturers. Meeting a supplier in person at a trade fair is a meaningful signal of their export seriousness.

**Referrals:** If you know another importer who sources packaging from India (not necessarily corrugated boxes - any category), ask who they use and whether they would refer them. Referrals are the highest-quality lead source.

## Manufacturer vs Broker: The Critical Distinction

Many entities listed as "corrugated box manufacturers" on IndiaMART are trading companies or brokers - they do not own a factory. They source from manufacturers, add a margin, and resell. This is not inherently problematic for some product categories, but for corrugated boxes it creates several issues:

- You lose direct visibility into the production process and quality controls
- Lead times can be unpredictable (the broker is depending on a factory they do not control)
- Certifications may be from the factory, but quality control is the broker's, which is weaker
- You typically pay more than factory-direct pricing

**How to identify a manufacturer:**
- Ask for the factory address and Google it - is there an industrial building there?
- Ask for a video call or virtual tour of the production floor showing the corrugator line and die-cutting equipment. A corrugator (the machine that makes corrugated board from liner and medium paper rolls) is a large, expensive, unmistakable piece of machinery.
- Ask for GST registration - manufacturers will have a GST number in the state where they manufacture. Cross-reference the GST number at gst.gov.in.
- Ask for a copy of their IE (Import Export) code certificate, which is required for any entity to export from India. Genuine manufacturers will have one.

## Key Questions to Ask a Potential Manufacturer

Once you have a shortlist of potential manufacturers, the qualification process involves asking the right questions and requiring evidence, not just assertions.

### Production capacity and capabilities

"What is your daily or monthly corrugated box production capacity?"
A credible mid-size manufacturer will produce 50,000 to 300,000 square metres of corrugated board per month. Smaller operations produce less; larger factories significantly more. An answer of "whatever you need" without specifics is a red flag.

"What flute types do you manufacture?"
You want B, C, E, and BC double wall as a minimum. For export work, C flute and BC double wall are most commonly required. See our [flute type guide](/blogs/b-flute-vs-c-flute-vs-e-flute-corrugated-boxes) for context.

"What printing methods do you have in-house?"
RSI flexographic printing (up to 4 colours) is standard. Offset printing on corrugated requires litho-lamination (printing on paperboard, then laminating to corrugated) and is less common in-house. For [custom printed corrugated boxes](/corrugated-boxes/custom-printed), confirm whether printing is done in-house or outsourced.

"What are your standard box styles?"
RSC (Regular Slotted Container) is the most common. A competent manufacturer should also produce Full Overlap (FOL), Half Slotted Container (HSC), and die-cut boxes. Ask specifically about the style you need.

### Quality and testing

"Can you provide test reports for board specifications?"
A manufacturer who tests their own production should be able to provide:
- GSM (grams per square metre) for each paper grade used
- Burst Strength (Mullen test) for finished board
- ECT (Edge Crush Test) for finished board
- BCT (Box Compression Test) for finished boxes at specific dimensions

"What is your rejection or defect rate?"
No good answer here except a specific, measured figure. A manufacturer who cannot tell you their defect rate is not measuring it, which means they are not managing it.

"Do you have ISO 9001 certification?"
ISO 9001 is a quality management system certification, not a product quality guarantee, but it indicates that the factory has documented processes and is audited by an external body. It is a meaningful baseline.

### Export experience

"Which countries have you exported to, and how many export orders have you completed in the last 12 months?"
Corrugated box export from India requires Phytosanitary certification (for wooden pallet packaging), proper packing list and commercial invoice documentation, and knowledge of import requirements in the destination country. A manufacturer with genuine export experience handles this routinely.

"Can you provide references from US or UK buyers?"
This is a reasonable request for any significant order. A manufacturer with no willingness to provide references should be treated with caution.

## The Sample Process: What to Expect and What to Ask For

Before placing a production order, always request samples. Here is how to run the sample process effectively:

**Request structural samples first:** Ask for plain (unprinted) samples in your specified dimensions, flute type, and ply count. Inspect the samples for:
- Internal dimensions (measure with a steel tape - allow for board thickness)
- Flap overlap (RSC flaps should meet in the centre, not gap or overlap significantly)
- Board stiffness (the panel should resist moderate hand pressure without denting)
- Delamination (press a fingernail into the board edge - the liner should not separate from the fluted medium)

**Request test reports with the samples:** Ask for the GSM certificate for the board used in your samples, the burst strength test result, and the ECT rating. A manufacturer who cannot produce these alongside samples is not testing to the standard you need.

**Test the samples yourself:** If your products are going into Amazon FBA, ISTA transit testing, or retailer vendor compliance programmes, test your sample boxes the same way. Fill them to operating weight, seal them with production tape, and put them through a drop test. This is the only way to know whether the specification is correct for your product.

**Request a print proof before production:** If you are printing, always approve a physical print proof before production run. Colour matching on flexographic printing from India is generally good but not offset-quality; approve what you can live with before committing.

## Red Flags to Watch For

**Price significantly below all competitors:** Corrugated board has defined raw material costs (paper). A quote dramatically lower than your other quotes is either a different (lower) specification, or it is a manufacturer who will cut corners somewhere in the production process. Always ask a low-price supplier to specify exactly which paper grades they are using.

**Unwillingness to provide test certificates:** If a manufacturer resists providing GSM, burst strength, and ECT test reports, they are either not testing or the results are below your specification. Both are disqualifying.

**No direct factory contact:** If all communication goes through a single salesperson who cannot answer technical questions about their production process, you may be talking to a broker.

**Unusually short lead times:** Corrugated boxes take time to produce. If a manufacturer quotes 5 working days for a 10,000-unit order that other manufacturers quote 20 working days for, something is wrong. They may already have the boxes in stock (which means they are not your specification) or they are overpromising.

**Vague or generic communication about export documentation:** A manufacturer experienced in export should be specific about what documents they provide: commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, bill of lading, phytosanitary certificate where required. Vague responses indicate inexperience.

## Certifications That Add Value for Export

For corrugated boxes going to US or UK buyers, the following certifications are commercially relevant:

**ISO 9001:** Quality management system. Standard for reputable manufacturers.

**FSC (Forest Stewardship Council):** Certifies that the virgin fibre content in the liner paper comes from responsibly managed forests. Increasingly required by retailers and brands with sustainability commitments.

**BIS (Bureau of Indian Standards):** For corrugated boxes specifically, BIS IS 1060 covers the Indian standard for fibreboard boxes. Most export-focused manufacturers test to TAPPI/ASTM standards instead, which are internationally recognised.

**ISTA protocols:** Internationally recognised transit testing protocols (ISTA 2A, 3A, etc.) that verify boxes can survive typical shipping conditions. A manufacturer who has produced boxes tested to ISTA standards can help you specify board grade to pass your specific protocol.

## Putting It Together: A Practical Checklist

Before placing a production order with any Indian corrugated box manufacturer:

Confirm they are a manufacturer, not a broker (factory address, corrugator machinery visible, GST registration cross-checked).

Confirm they have genuine export experience (IE code, references, export history in trade databases).

Receive and inspect physical samples in your specified dimension and board grade.

Receive and verify test reports: GSM, burst strength, ECT.

Approve a print proof if printing is included.

Confirm lead time, payment terms, and shipping Incoterms in writing.

Confirm what export documents are provided and who arranges freight.
