When US and UK importers start sourcing corrugated boxes from India, the first question is usually about pricing. The second, often asked after a frustrating back-and-forth, is: "Why did the quote change?" Almost always, the answer comes back to the same issue - incomplete specifications sent with the initial inquiry.

A corrugated box is not a commodity with a catalogue price. It is a manufactured product built to a specific combination of dimensions, board grade, flute type, and print specification. Without those inputs, a manufacturer can only guess. This guide covers exactly what to prepare so that the first quote you get from an Indian supplier is an accurate one.

## The Seven Specifications Every Corrugated Box Quote Needs

### 1. Internal Dimensions (L x W x H)

Always specify internal dimensions - the space inside the box, not the outer footprint. This is where your product sits. If you give external dimensions, the manufacturer subtracts the board thickness to get internal dimensions, which introduces guessing.

State dimensions in millimetres or inches. For a regular slotted carton (RSC), the convention is Length x Width x Height where Length is the longest base dimension. For unusual box styles (full overlap, half-slotted, tray), specify the style by name as well.

### 2. Flute Type

The flute type determines the board's cushioning performance, compression strength, and printability. The standard export options are:

- **B flute:** 3mm, stiff surface, good for retail display and canned goods
- **C flute:** 4mm, cushioning plus printability, the default for general sea freight
- **E flute:** 1.5mm, very smooth, for premium printed retail boxes
- **BC double wall:** 7mm, 5-ply, for heavy goods requiring high stack strength

If you are not sure which flute you need, state your product weight, dimensions, and how the boxes will be stacked in transit. An experienced supplier will recommend a construction and produce a sample in the recommended spec alongside any alternative you request. Our guide on [B, C, and E flute corrugated boxes](/blogs/b-flute-vs-c-flute-vs-e-flute-corrugated-boxes) explains each profile in detail.

### 3. Liner Grade or Strength Requirement

Two boxes can use the same C flute with completely different strength ratings depending on the liner grade. A 100 GSM test liner and a 150 GSM kraftliner are both C flute but the burst strength and BCT (Box Compression Test) result will differ significantly.

You have two ways to specify this:
- **By liner grade:** state the liner GSM (e.g., 100 GSM test liner / 150 GSM kraftliner) or the board specification your current supplier uses
- **By performance requirement:** state the minimum burst factor (BF) or BCT value your product or retailer requires

If you have a vendor compliance requirement from Amazon, Walmart, Target, or a UK retailer, share it with your supplier. It determines the board grade.

### 4. Quantity

Order quantity affects everything: price per unit, production scheduling, and which printing method is feasible. State your quantity in units for the initial inquiry. For first orders, also state whether you expect to repeat the order and at what frequency - this helps the supplier set up tooling and die-cutting in a way that minimises future costs.

Most Indian manufacturers have a minimum order quantity of 5,000 units. Below that threshold, some will produce for sampling purposes but not for commercial supply.

### 5. Printing Specification

Unprinted boxes (plain kraft) are the simplest. Printed boxes require additional information:

- Number of print colours
- Print placement (one face only, all faces, inside bottom)
- Any specific colour codes (Pantone, RAL) or if standard flexographic colour matching is acceptable
- Whether the print includes barcodes or regulatory text at specific sizes

If you have existing artwork, share the files at the inquiry stage. It allows the supplier to quote accurately and flag any technical constraints before production.

### 6. Delivery Destination

State your destination port and Incoterms preference. The most common for factory-direct orders from India are FOB Mundra or FOB Nhava Sheva (for Gujarat and Mumbai-based manufacturers) and CIF USA or CIF UK (where the supplier arranges freight to destination port).

Delivery destination affects the freight cost component of any landed-cost calculation and determines which documentary requirements apply (e.g., US FDA registration for food-contact packaging, UK UKCA requirements for certain applications).

### 7. Target Timeline

State when you need the boxes at destination. This allows the supplier to confirm whether your timeline is achievable given current production loading, and to advise on the latest acceptable order date based on sea freight transit times. From India, standard sea freight to the US East Coast takes 22 to 28 days; to the UK, 18 to 22 days.

## What a Good Quote From India Should Include

![Sea freight containers being loaded at an Indian port for export to USA and UK](https://images.pexels.com/photos/906982/pexels-photo-906982.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)
*Most corrugated box export orders from India ship by sea freight. A good quote specifies the Incoterms and freight cost separately so you can see the full landed cost.*

A professional quote from an Indian corrugated box manufacturer should cover the following items. If any are missing, ask for them before proceeding:

- **Unit price** per box at the quoted quantity, with Incoterms clearly stated (e.g., FOB Mundra, USD per unit)
- **Board specification** confirmed in the quote (flute type, ply count, liner GSM or burst factor)
- **Production lead time** from payment or sample approval to cargo ready date
- **Minimum order quantity** for the quoted specification
- **Sample availability** - can samples be produced before the production order, and at what cost
- **Payment terms** - typical for Indian exporters is 30% advance, 70% against Bill of Lading copy
- **Quality documentation** available - burst strength test certificates, GSM verification, pre-shipment inspection options

Any quote that does not include the board specification in writing should be treated as provisional. You need the spec confirmed before you can compare it against another quote or verify it against your requirements.

## Common Mistakes US and UK Importers Make When Requesting Quotes

**Sending only external dimensions.** As described above, this introduces errors. Always send internal dimensions.

**Not specifying flute type.** Some suppliers will default to the cheapest available board grade if no specification is given. You may get a working quote but not a quote for the box you actually need.

**Comparing quotes with different specs.** If one supplier quotes C flute / 150 GSM kraftliner and another quotes C flute / 100 GSM test liner, the unit prices are not comparable. Always confirm the board spec before comparing prices.

**Ignoring sample approval.** Producing a sample in your exact dimensions and board grade before the production run is standard practice in Indian export manufacturing. Suppliers who skip straight to production without a signed sample approval are cutting a step that protects both parties.

**Not asking about quality documentation.** Burst strength certificates, GSM certificates, and pre-shipment inspection reports are standard with every professional Indian corrugated box export order. If a supplier does not offer them, it is a signal worth noting.

## What Happens After You Send a Specification

Understanding the typical sequence helps set realistic expectations and reduces the back-and-forth that slows most first-time import inquiries.

**Day 1 to 2: Initial review and clarifications**
The manufacturer reviews your specification and comes back with any gaps. Common omissions in first-time inquiries: no Incoterms preference stated, flute type not specified, print colours described as "our brand blue" without a Pantone reference. A fast response to these clarifications keeps the timeline moving.

**Day 2 to 5: Quote and sample quote issued**
You typically receive two figures - a sample cost (usually for 50 to 100 units, air-shipped to you) and a production cost at your stated quantity. Sample costs are often partially subsidised for first-time buyers on the understanding that a production order follows.

**Day 5 to 12: Sample production and shipment**
Samples are produced and air-shipped, typically taking 5 to 7 business days from confirmation. You check the sample against your product: does it fit cleanly, does the print match your colour references, does the board feel right for the application. Any adjustments - a dimension change, a different liner grade, a print colour correction - are fed back for a revised sample at minimal additional cost.

**Day 12 to 15: Production order confirmation**
Once a sample is approved in writing, the production order is confirmed with the purchase order and advance payment. The production queue is scheduled and a cargo ready date is confirmed in writing.

This pre-production phase typically takes two to three weeks for a straightforward specification. Complex custom die-cut styles or four-colour print can extend it by a week. Factor this into your planning when the boxes have a firm arrival deadline.

## Retailer and Marketplace Compliance Requirements

If your corrugated boxes will enter an Amazon fulfilment centre, a Walmart vendor programme, or a UK retail distribution centre, the specification that qualifies your boxes for your own warehouse may not qualify them for your retail customer. Check these requirements before finalising your quote.

**Amazon FBA corrugated box requirements**
Amazon's fulfilment centre receiving requirements specify minimum burst strength and box compression test (BCT) values depending on the packed product weight. For products up to 50 lbs, standard C flute single wall generally meets requirements; for heavier products, double wall BC is often needed. Amazon also sets requirements on maximum outer dimensions, labelling placement, and pallet overhang.

If you are an Amazon FBA seller sourcing boxes from India, share the relevant Amazon packaging compliance guidance with your manufacturer at the quote stage. Our [Amazon FBA corrugated boxes guide](/blogs/amazon-fba-corrugated-boxes-india) covers Indian-manufactured box specifications for FBA compliance in detail.

**Walmart and Target vendor packaging requirements**
These retailers issue vendor compliance manuals that specify corrugated box performance standards. Common requirements include minimum ECT (edge crush test) values, maximum pallet overhangs, and specific labelling zones. Chargebacks for non-compliant packaging are a real cost - specifying correctly upfront is cheaper than failing compliance on arrival.

**UK retail distribution requirements**
UK distribution centres often specify BSEN ISO 2247 drop test requirements or internal vendor standards. The key compliance point for most UK importers is confirming that the burst strength or BCT values quoted by your Indian supplier are measured under the same test standards as the values your customer's manual references. Indian manufacturers familiar with UK export use ASTM and ISO standards and can cross-reference these for most UK retailer requirements on request.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long does a corrugated box quote from India take to prepare?**
For a complete specification, a professional manufacturer will return a quote within 24 to 48 hours. Incomplete specifications requiring clarification extend this. The fastest way to get a quote is to send all seven specification inputs listed in this guide in your first message.

**Can I get samples before committing to a production order?**
Yes. Most established Indian corrugated box manufacturers will produce samples in your exact specification. Samples are typically 50 to 100 units, air-shipped to you, with a lead time of 5 to 7 working days from confirmation. There is usually a sample cost, which may be credited against your first production order.

**What payment terms should I expect from an Indian manufacturer?**
The industry standard is 30% advance against purchase order and 70% against bill of lading copy. Established buyers with a track record of repeat orders may negotiate different terms over time, but 30/70 is the starting norm and protects both parties.

**Is it possible to compare quotes from multiple Indian suppliers?**
Yes, and it is sensible to do so for a first order. The important point is to ensure the quotes are for identical specifications - the same flute, the same liner GSM, the same print specification. A C flute / 150 GSM kraftliner quote is not comparable with a C flute / 100 GSM test liner quote even if the unit price appears similar. Always confirm the board specification before comparing prices.

**What documents does an Indian corrugated box export shipment include?**
A standard export shipment from India includes: commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading (or airway bill), and certificate of origin. For buyers who request them, quality documents include burst strength and GSM test certificates. A fumigation certificate is available where required by the destination country, and a third-party pre-shipment inspection report can be arranged through SGS, Bureau Veritas, or Intertek.

**What is the minimum order for a corrugated box quote to be commercially relevant?**
Most Indian manufacturers quote commercially from 5,000 units upwards. At lower quantities, the economics of a custom production run, tooling, and sea freight rarely produce competitive landed unit costs compared to domestic sourcing. Some manufacturers will produce smaller quantities for sampling purposes only.
