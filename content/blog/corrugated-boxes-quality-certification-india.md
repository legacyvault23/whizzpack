One of the most common concerns US and UK buyers have about sourcing corrugated boxes from India is quality verification. How do you know the box you receive matches what you specified? How do you confirm the board grade is what the factory claims? And what do you do if boxes arrive and they are not right?

The answers are straightforward, and quality verification for Indian corrugated boxes is not materially different from verifying domestic supply. The difference is that you are doing it remotely, which requires knowing what to ask for and how to read what you receive.

---

## The Three Quality Tests That Matter

Corrugated board quality is measured with three tests. Understanding what each one measures tells you which one to require for your application.

### Burst Strength (Mullen Test)

**What it measures:** The pressure the corrugated board can withstand before the liner ruptures. Expressed in kg/cm2 (kilograms per square centimetre) in India, or PSI (pounds per square inch) in the US, or kPa (kilopascals) in the UK.

**When it matters:** Burst strength is the relevant test when boxes face rough handling, puncture from sharp internal contents, or significant lateral pressure during packing. If your boxes contain metal parts, tools, or any product with sharp corners or edges, burst strength is the primary specification.

**Typical values for export-grade corrugated from India:**

| Construction | Typical Burst Strength |
|-------------|----------------------|
| Single wall, 150/120/150 GSM | 10 to 14 kg/cm2 |
| Single wall, 180/150/180 GSM | 14 to 18 kg/cm2 |
| Double wall BC | 20 to 28 kg/cm2 |
| Triple wall | 35 to 50 kg/cm2 |

**Conversion:** 1 kg/cm2 = 14.22 PSI = 98.07 kPa

### Edge Crush Test (ECT)

**What it measures:** The load the corrugated board can carry before the flute columns collapse under vertical (stacking) load. Expressed in kN/m (kilonewtons per metre) or lb/in (pounds per linear inch).

**When it matters:** ECT is the more relevant test for sea freight export, because boxes in a container are stacked under vertical compression for 3 to 4 weeks. A box that passes a high burst strength test but has low ECT will be crushed by the weight of boxes above it before it reaches port.

**Required ECT values for common export applications:**

| Application | Minimum ECT |
|-------------|-------------|
| Light goods, under 10 kg per box | 4 to 5 kN/m (23 ECT lb/in) |
| General export, 10 to 25 kg | 6 to 7 kN/m (32 ECT lb/in) |
| Heavy goods, 25 to 50 kg | 8 to 10 kN/m (44 ECT lb/in) |
| Industrial/heavy export | 12 kN/m and above (60+ ECT lb/in) |

Specify ECT by minimum value on your purchase order - not just "double wall" or "heavy grade." These terms are not standardised and mean different things to different factories.

### GSM (Grams per Square Metre)

**What it measures:** The weight of each paper layer in the corrugated board construction. GSM is a direct indicator of paper quality - heavier paper has more fibre content and is stronger. Corrugated board is specified by the GSM of each layer: outer liner / corrugated medium / inner liner (and additional layers for multi-wall boards).

**Common board grade notations from Indian manufacturers:**

| Grade | Outer Liner | Medium | Inner Liner | Total |
|-------|-------------|--------|-------------|-------|
| Light | 120 GSM | 90 GSM | 120 GSM | 330 GSM |
| Standard | 150 GSM | 120 GSM | 150 GSM | 420 GSM |
| Heavy export | 180 GSM | 150 GSM | 180 GSM | 510 GSM |
| Extra heavy | 200 GSM | 180 GSM | 200 GSM | 580 GSM |

GSM certificates are tested on samples from the production batch. They confirm the factory used the specified paper weight and did not substitute a lighter grade.

## How to Read an Indian Quality Test Certificate

Indian corrugated manufacturers typically use a standardised test report format. Here is what each section contains:

**Certificate header:** Factory name, certificate number, date of test, client/order reference.

**Sample description:** Box dimensions, construction (single/double wall, flute type), board specification (GSM grade), quantity manufactured.

**Test results section:**
- Bursting Strength (BS): Given in kg/cm2. Compare against your specification.
- Edge Crush Test (ECT) or Ring Crush Test (RCT): Given in kN/m or kg/cm. RCT is an alternative to ECT common in Indian testing - it measures the same property.
- GSM of each layer: Confirms paper weights match the specified grade.
- Moisture content: Expressed as a percentage. Should be 7 to 9% for export-grade board; higher moisture content indicates the board may have been stored in humid conditions and will have lower effective strength.

**Signatory and stamp:** The certificate should be signed by the quality control officer and stamped with the factory's quality seal. An unsigned or unstamped certificate is not valid for customs or compliance purposes.

**Test method reference:** Reputable Indian manufacturers test to BIS (Bureau of Indian Standards) methods, which align with ISO and TAPPI standards recognised internationally. Common method references: IS 1968 (bursting strength), IS 2365 (ECT), IS 1060 (GSM).

## How to Request Quality Certificates from an Indian Supplier

At the sample stage: "Please provide burst strength and ECT test certificates for the board batch used in our sample."

At the purchase order stage: "Please include burst strength, ECT, and GSM certificates for the production batch in the shipment documents."

A factory that cannot or will not provide these certificates is not ready for international export. This is a hard filter, not a negotiating point.

If the factory says their certificates are "available on request after delivery," that is not acceptable. Certificates should accompany the shipment or be emailed before the vessel departs.

## Pre-Shipment Inspection

For large first orders (a full container or more), a pre-shipment inspection by a third-party quality inspection firm is worth considering. Firms such as Bureau Veritas, Intertek, SGS, and QIMA operate in India's major industrial cities and can perform a physical inspection of your production run before loading.

A standard corrugated box pre-shipment inspection covers:
- Random sampling of production cartons against specification
- Dimensional verification
- Board grade confirmation (GSM and visual flute inspection)
- Print quality check
- Carton compression test on samples

The cost is typically $250 to $450 USD per inspection day, which is a small fraction of the value of a full container order.

## What to Do If Boxes Arrive Out of Specification

Despite best efforts, quality issues do occur. When boxes arrive from India and are out of specification, the correct approach is:

**Document immediately:** Photograph the issue clearly - dimensional deviations, board failures, print errors, structural defects. Measure and record specific deviations against the approved specification.

**Notify the factory in writing within 14 days of receipt.** Most Indian manufacturers accept quality claims within 28 to 30 days of delivery; beyond this window, claims are harder to resolve.

**Provide a quantified claim:** "X units out of Y total are out of specification. Specific defect is [describe]. This resulted in [quantified business impact]." A vague claim results in a slow response. A specific, documented claim gets resolved faster.

**Discuss remedies:** Indian manufacturers typically offer a credit on the next order, replacement production, or a partial refund, depending on the defect severity and the commercial relationship. Full refunds on delivered goods are rare and generally require significant, documented defects.

**Preventive approach:** The best response to a quality dispute is making sure it does not happen. Request pre-production samples, verify test certificates, and for large orders, commission a pre-shipment inspection. These steps together reduce the probability of a quality dispute to near zero.
