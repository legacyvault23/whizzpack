The snack food category has one of the most demanding secondary packaging briefs in FMCG. A bag of crisps, a case of protein bars, or a pallet of granola pouches has to survive a supply chain from factory floor to retail shelf or consumer doorstep - without damage, without losing shelf presence, and increasingly without generating unnecessary packaging waste.

Corrugated secondary packaging is the workhorse of that supply chain. It is the transit outer that protects product in shipping, the shelf-ready display case that determines how product lands on the retail fixture, and the branded shipper box that DTC snack brands use to create an unboxing moment at the doorstep. For UK and US snack food brands looking to reduce packaging costs without reducing quality, India has become a serious sourcing option.

This guide covers what snack food brands actually need to specify when sourcing corrugated boxes from India, which certifications matter for retail buyers and UK EPR compliance, and what to expect on MOQ, lead times, and quality assurance. ## Two Formats, Different Briefs: Shipping Outers vs Shelf-Ready Packaging

### Shipping Outers and Transit Boxes

Most snack food brands start with a single need: a corrugated outer that holds a given number of retail units, survives palletisation and freight transit, and stacks safely in a warehouse or distribution centre.

The specification brief for a shipping outer is relatively straightforward:

**Box style:** Regular Slotted Container (RSC) is the standard. RSC boxes close top and bottom with flaps, are straightforward to pack by hand or machine, and are cost-effective to manufacture at scale.

**Board grade:** For snack food products sealed in primary packaging (heat-sealed film pouches, foil bags, wrappers), a B-flute single-wall at 150 to 200 GSM liner is adequate for most transit requirements. Products with higher stacking loads, or with moisture risk in transit, benefit from C-flute with higher burst strength.

**Internal dimensions:** Specify to the millimetre. Measure your primary pack dimensions and how units will be arranged inside the outer (2x4, 3x4, etc.). Factory-direct manufacturers can cut to any dimension at no additional tooling cost for RSC styles.

**Print:** RSC shipping outers are typically plain or one-colour flexo-printed with brand name, product name, lot code panel, and handling instructions. A second colour is achievable at modest cost.

For a detailed comparison of B-flute and C-flute performance differences in snack food secondary packaging, our [guide to B-flute, C-flute, and E-flute corrugated boxes](/blogs/b-flute-vs-c-flute-vs-e-flute-corrugated-boxes) covers the trade-offs in strength, printability, and weight for each format.

### Shelf-Ready Packaging (SRP)

Shelf-ready packaging (also called retail-ready packaging, or RRP) is a corrugated format designed to move from the stockroom to the retail shelf without repacking. UK grocery retailers including Tesco, Sainsbury's, ASDA, and Waitrose increasingly require SRP compliance for ambient snack categories as a condition of supply.

SRP boxes are engineered differently from standard shipping outers:

**Perforated tear strip:** A precisely scored perforation runs around three sides of the box, typically at a height that leaves a low tray with the product visible. The shelf-fill team tears the strip to convert the shipping case directly into a display tray.

**Printed front panel:** The front face of the box (the panel facing the shopper once opened) is print-finished with the brand logo, product name, and shelf talker information. This print face is often the consumer's first visual cue on the shelf.

**Base and side panel strength:** The remaining tray needs enough rigidity to hold product weight without bowing during the stock rotation period. Double-wall base construction or a reinforced base score is common for heavier snack formats.

**Print registration:** Because the front panel is the shelf display face, print registration on SRP boxes needs to be held to tighter tolerances than a standard shipping outer.

A well-specified SRP box reduces shelf-fill labour time, improves on-shelf compliance, and removes the need for a separate display unit. UK grocery buyers treat SRP capability as a baseline expectation from mid-tier brands and above.

## Board Grade and Food Safety for Snack Packaging

Most snack food products (crisps, granola, protein bars, biscuits, dried fruit, nuts, energy bars, crackers) are sealed in primary packaging before they go into a corrugated outer. The corrugated box is secondary packaging: it does not contact food directly.

This means food safety requirements for corrugated secondary packaging are less demanding than for primary contact materials. There are, however, a few areas where snack food brands should pay attention.

### FSC Certification

UK retailers with sustainability commitments, and an increasing number of US retailers, require FSC chain-of-custody certified packaging as a supplier compliance condition. FSC certification confirms the fibre in the corrugated board came from responsibly managed forests or recycled sources. Indian corrugated manufacturers who export to UK and European buyers typically hold FSC chain-of-custody certification. Request the certificate number and verify it at info.fsc.org before confirming an order.

### Recycled Content

High-recycled-content board is standard in the Indian corrugated industry. Boards using 70 to 100% post-consumer waste fibre are available, biodegradable, fully recyclable through standard paper and cardboard collection streams, and acceptable for most retail buyer sustainability requirements. For UK brands managing Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) reporting, your supplier should be able to provide a material data sheet specifying recycled content percentage by layer.

### Ink and Coating Specification

Corrugated boxes for food brands should be printed with water-based inks rather than solvent-based inks. Water-based inks do not off-gas solvents in a closed secondary packaging environment and keep the board fully recyclable at end of life. Request a written ink specification from your supplier. Any wax or plastic coating (sometimes used to add moisture resistance) makes the board non-recyclable and should be avoided unless absolutely required for the application.

## Custom Printing: Getting the Shelf Presence Right

The corrugated box is increasingly a brand touchpoint, not just a container. For snack food brands, there are two moments where print quality matters most: the SRP front panel (retailer-facing) and the e-commerce shipper (consumer-facing).

### Flexographic Printing

Flexographic printing is the standard for corrugated packaging. It prints directly onto the corrugated board surface using flexible rubber plates. Constraints to be aware of:

- Fine lines and small text below 6pt are difficult to reproduce cleanly on corrugated surfaces
- Photographic gradients reproduce poorly - use solid colour fills and bold graphics for best results
- Colour matching to brand standards (Pantone references) is achievable with experienced operators but requires a press proof run before full production

### High-Definition Flexo and Litho-Laminate

For premium snack brands where the outer carton needs to match the quality of retail shelf primary packaging, high-definition flexo printing or litho-laminate construction is available from specialist Indian manufacturers:

- **High-definition flexo** achieves finer dot reproduction and closer Pantone matching than standard flexo
- **Litho-laminate** involves a pre-printed lithographic sheet laminated onto corrugated board, giving near-offset print quality on a corrugated base

Both options add cost and lead time but are appropriate for premium retail ranges where the box is a consumer-facing brand element.

### E-Commerce Branded Shippers

For DTC snack brands, the shipper box is the unboxing experience. Custom-printed single-wall corrugated boxes with full-colour or spot-colour exterior print, combined with a kraft or white liner interior, are the most common format. Box styles include RSC (most economical), mailer-style with tuck-in lid (better opening experience), and die-cut display boxes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What corrugated box specification should a snack food brand use for shelf-ready packaging?**

For shelf-ready packaging (SRP), snack food brands typically use B-flute or C-flute single-wall construction with a perforated tear strip scored at shelf tray height and a printed front panel for on-shelf visibility. B-flute gives a flatter board surface for better print quality; C-flute gives higher stacking strength for heavier snack formats. The base of the remaining tray should be reinforced if product weight exceeds 2 to 3 kg per display tray. Print specification should prioritise solid colour fills and bold graphics rather than photographic gradients, which do not reproduce cleanly on standard flexo corrugated print.

**Do corrugated boxes from India meet UK retailer FSC requirements?**

Yes. Established Indian corrugated exporters hold FSC chain-of-custody certification covering their board supply. FSC chain-of-custody certification confirms the fibre came from responsibly managed forests or recycled sources and is the standard certification required by UK grocery retailers including Tesco, Sainsbury's, Waitrose, and ASDA for packaging sustainability requirements. Request the certificate number from your supplier and verify it at info.fsc.org before confirming an order.

**Can Indian manufacturers produce corrugated boxes suitable for food brand retailer audits?**

For corrugated secondary packaging where the box does not contact food directly, the key compliance documents are an FSC chain-of-custody certificate, a recycled content declaration, and an ink specification confirming water-based inks. These cover the requirements for most UK and US food brand retailer audits. For any application where corrugated board may contact food directly, ask specifically for a food-contact compliance declaration and confirm the board grade meets the relevant standard.

**What is the minimum order quantity for corrugated snack food packaging from India?**

The minimum order quantity at Whizzpack is 5,000 units per specification (per size, board grade, and print). Multi-SKU orders for brands with several box sizes can combine quantities across SKUs to reach the minimum. For a first order, we recommend treating the initial run as a specification-confirmation order: sample first to confirm dimensions, board quality, and SRP tear-strip performance, then place the first production run at full quantities once the specification is locked.

**How does sourcing corrugated boxes from India compare to a local UK supplier for a snack food brand?**

Indian manufacturers offer significantly lower unit cost for medium-to-large production runs, full custom specification capability (any size, board grade, custom SRP engineering, full-colour print), and strong FSC and recycled content credentials. The trade-off is lead time: an Indian supplier requires 8 to 12 weeks total for a first order versus a few days from a local UK supplier. The practical approach for snack food brands is to use Indian suppliers for planned seasonal or new product launch runs, placed 14 to 16 weeks in advance, and maintain a small buffer stock from a local supplier for reactive top-up needs.
