## Corrugated Packaging for Toy and Games Brands

The global toy market was valued at over $100 billion in 2024. The UK toy market alone exceeds £3.5 billion annually. And behind every board game, wooden toy set, and construction kit sold through UK and US retail is a corrugated secondary packaging supply chain that brands rarely think about until it causes a problem.

Toy and games packaging has specific requirements that differ from general retail corrugated. Print quality matters more here than in most categories - a toy on a Smyths or Target shelf competes visually against dozens of competitors, and the box is often the primary marketing asset. Safety labelling requirements add regulatory complexity. Sustainability pressure from retailers is increasingly non-negotiable.

This guide covers how toy and games brands should specify corrugated packaging when sourcing from India, what board grades and print options to use, and what regulatory requirements affect the packaging itself.

## Types of Corrugated Packaging Toy Brands Use

Toy and games brands typically need several distinct corrugated formats, often ordered together but specified separately:

**Retail inner packaging** is the printed box the consumer picks off the shelf. For most toy categories, this is a high-print corrugated box in E flute (1.5mm) or B flute (3mm) that carries the brand, product imagery, safety information, age grading, and barcode. The box is the product presentation at point of sale. It must print cleanly, hold its shape under retail handling, and meet the sustainability requirements of the retailer.

**Master shipping carton** is the outer box used to pack multiple retail units for warehouse storage and transport. This is typically 3-ply B flute or 5-ply BC flute, plain kraft or with minimal print (SKU, quantity, handling instructions). Compression strength is the key specification here - master cartons are palletised and stacked, often in refrigerated or humidity-controlled containers on long sea freight routes.

**Shelf-ready packaging (SRP)** allows the retailer to cut a perforation and place the inner tray directly on the shelf. Widely used in the toys category at grocery multiples (Tesco, Asda, Walmart) and toy specialists (Smyths, Toys R Us). B flute or E flute depending on the weight of the product; the SRP tray must be rigid enough to support the product without a back panel once opened.

**Display cases and floor stands** are used for impulse-purchase categories: small toys, collectible figures, trading card packs, and seasonal products. These are usually E flute, heavily printed, with die-cut windows to show product.

**DTC and subscription mailer boxes** are used by toy brands selling direct-to-consumer or through subscription boxes. E flute, fitted to the product, with the brand printed on the exterior. The unboxing experience is a significant design consideration for DTC toy brands. ## Board Grades for Toy and Games Packaging

The board grade decision in toy packaging is driven by two competing requirements: print quality (which favours thinner, smoother board) and structural strength (which favours thicker, denser board).

| Format | Board spec | Flute | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Retail inner box (lightweight toy) | 3-ply | E flute | Best print surface, sharp colours |
| Retail inner box (heavy or large toy) | 3-ply | B flute | More rigidity for larger formats |
| Shelf-ready tray | 3-ply | B or E flute | Depends on product weight |
| Master carton (standard) | 3-ply | B flute | Most common for toys under 5kg |
| Master carton (heavy items) | 5-ply | BC flute | Board games, heavy construction sets |
| Display case | 3-ply | E flute | Max print area, light product only |
| Mailer box (DTC) | 3-ply | E flute | Fitted to product, unboxing focus |

E flute at 1.5mm provides the smoothest surface for full-colour printing. Its tighter corrugation gives a clean flexo print and handles 4-colour process printing well. The trade-off is lower stacking strength - E flute is not suitable for heavy product or stacked master carton applications.

B flute at 3mm is the standard for most retail corrugated in the toy category. It provides a good balance of rigidity, print surface, and cost. For board games and heavier toys, BC double wall (5-ply) provides the compression strength needed to survive a palletised sea freight journey from India to the UK or US.

## Print Requirements for Toy Retail Packaging

Toy retail packaging is one of the most print-intensive categories in corrugated. Brands compete visually on shelf, and the packaging must render brand colours, character artwork, and product photography accurately and consistently across production runs.

**Flexographic printing** is the standard for toy corrugated. Modern flexo in 4-6 colours delivers vibrant results on E flute board. Plate costs are a one-time investment per design; subsequent runs have no plate cost. For brands with multiple SKUs that share a structural format but differ in design (different characters, game editions, product variants), flexo is the most cost-effective approach once past the initial setup.

**Digital printing** is used for smaller runs, product launches, and SKUs with frequent design updates. No plate cost, full colour, minimum runs of 1,000-2,000 units in most plants. The per-unit cost is higher than flexo at volume, but the absence of plate investment makes it economic for new brands or seasonal designs.

**Spot colours** matter in toy packaging more than in most categories. Brand colours for major toy IP (licensed character products, franchise games) are often specified as Pantone references and must match across production runs and between carton and inner packaging. Confirm with your manufacturer that they can hold tight colour tolerances on spot colours across runs.

**Finish options:** Water-based varnish over print provides a degree of scuff resistance and a subtle sheen. Matt laminate is used for premium toy and collector item packaging. Gloss laminate is common for character-branded toys. Note that laminate finishes affect recyclability - if your retail buyer requires "fully recyclable" packaging, specify water-based varnish or no coating. ## Safety Labelling on Toy Packaging

This is an area that catches many brands off guard when ordering corrugated from overseas manufacturers.

Toy packaging must carry specific safety information under UK and US regulations. This is the brand's responsibility, not the packaging manufacturer's - but it has direct implications for what must be printed on the corrugated.

**UK:** The UK Toy Safety Regulations 2011 (as retained post-Brexit, with UKCA marking replacing CE) require toys to carry the UKCA mark, the name and address of the UK responsible person, and age grading warnings where applicable. The minimum age warning must appear on the toy and its packaging. Packaging must also carry choking hazard warnings for products with small parts (under 3 years).

**EU (for brands also selling in Europe):** CE marking under the EU Toy Safety Directive (2009/48/EC). Equivalent requirements to the UK, with the CE mark and EU responsible person address on packaging.

**USA:** ASTM F963 (Consumer Product Safety Improvement Act) requires age grading and small parts warnings. The Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) specifies minimum warning label sizes and placements. The phrase "WARNING: CHOKING HAZARD - Small parts" in a specific format is required for products with small parts intended for ages 3 and up.

These requirements mean the print spec for retail toy packaging must be agreed with your brand's compliance team before the artwork is sent to the manufacturer. The Indian corrugated manufacturer prints what is in the artwork file - they do not advise on regulatory compliance. Have artwork signed off by your legal or compliance team before ordering.

## Sustainability Requirements from UK and US Retailers

The major UK and US toy retailers have made public commitments on sustainable packaging. Brands looking to list at Smyths, John Lewis, Tesco, Walmart, or Target increasingly need to meet these requirements:

**FSC certification:** The Forest Stewardship Council certification is now a baseline requirement or strong preference at most major UK and US toy retailers. FSC certifies the paper fibre in the corrugated board, not the design. Whizzpack holds FSC chain-of-custody certification, meaning boxes we manufacture can carry the FSC Mix or FSC 100% label. This is particularly relevant for toy brands targeting ethical and premium market positions.

**Recyclability:** Corrugated board is inherently recyclable and is one of the most recycled materials globally. However, plastic windows in toy packaging (die-cut windows with PVC or PET inserts to show the product) create recyclability issues. UK and EU packaging regulations are moving to reduce mixed-material packaging. If your product currently uses a plastic window, consider moving to a fully open-face display or a paper-based window as a more sustainable alternative.

**Reduced plastic:** Many toy brands are actively removing plastic from their secondary packaging as part of sustainability commitments. Corrugated inserts and paper tissue replace plastic bags and foam in many premium toy formats.

**Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR):** UK brands must register under the UK Packaging Regulations and report packaging placed on market. This is an administrative compliance obligation and does not constrain the corrugated specification, but brands must track the weight and material type of all packaging they import.

## Amazon FBA Requirements for Toy Brands

Toy brands selling through Amazon FBA in the UK and US need corrugated packaging that meets Amazon's fulfilment requirements. Key specifications:

**Prep requirements:** Amazon requires boxes to be labelled with FNSKU barcodes on the exterior of each unit. For toys in corrugated retail boxes, the FNSKU label is usually applied over the existing EAN/UPC barcode. Plan label placement in the original box design.

**Box strength:** Amazon FBA requires sufficient crush resistance to survive automated sorting and stacking in fulfilment centres. For standard toy products, 3-ply B flute with a minimum 32 ECT (edge crush test) specification is the standard. Heavy toys or products requiring extra protection should specify 5-ply.

**Mailer box compliance:** For DTC/FBA mailer formats, Amazon's packaging requirements specify that boxes must be free of excess dunnage, sized appropriately to the product (no more than 2 inches of empty space per side), and must not require additional outer packaging in most cases (ships in own container, or SIOC).

## Board Game Packaging: Specific Considerations

Board game packaging has requirements that differ from toy packaging in several ways worth calling out separately.

**Box depth and rigidity:** Board game boxes need to be rigid enough to hold their contents (cards, boards, pieces, rulebooks) without bowing or deforming on retail shelves. A 3mm B flute box with full-overlap top and bottom flaps (FOL construction) is the standard. Telescoping box construction (lid over base) is used for premium games.

**Weight:** A substantial board game can weigh 1-3kg. The master carton specification must account for this. 5-ply BC double wall is typically required for master cartons packing 6-12 board games, particularly if the boxes will be stacked four or five pallets high in a warehouse.

**Insert design:** Many board game publishers use corrugated inserts (custom die-cut trays) inside the main box to organise game components. These are typically E flute for clean fold edges and precise component fit. The insert design must be specced alongside the outer box - ideally from the same manufacturer to ensure dimensional consistency.

**Edition and variant management:** Board games frequently ship in multiple editions (standard, deluxe, anniversary), language variants, and expansion packs. Each is a separate SKU requiring a separate box design and print run. Managing this across production runs from India requires a clear SKU-level specification document.

When requesting a quote for toy or games corrugated packaging from an Indian manufacturer, include:

## FAQ

**What flute is used for toy retail packaging?**

E flute (1.5mm) is the most common for printed retail inner boxes in the toy category because it provides the smoothest print surface and the most accurate colour reproduction. B flute (3mm) is used for heavier or larger products where rigidity is more important than print quality. Master cartons use B flute or BC double wall.

**Can corrugated boxes carry the FSC label for retail toy products?**

Yes, provided the manufacturer holds FSC chain-of-custody certification. Whizzpack is FSC CoC certified. The FSC logo and associated text are placed in the artwork file by the brand and printed as part of the run. The manufacturer's FSC certificate number must be referenced on the artwork.

**What are the minimum print requirements for toy packaging under UK safety regulations?**

The UKCA mark, the name and address of the UK responsible person, and age grading warnings must appear on the packaging. Choking hazard warnings (small parts) must appear in specified minimum size and placement. These are brand responsibilities - the packaging manufacturer prints the artwork as supplied. Have artwork reviewed by your compliance team before sending to print.

**What is the minimum order for custom corrugated toy boxes from India?**

Minimum order at Whizzpack is 5,000 units per SKU. This applies to custom dimensions and print. Multiple SKUs require separate minimum orders per SKU.

**How long does it take to receive corrugated toy packaging from India?**

Production lead time is 18-28 days. Sea freight to the UK (Felixstowe) is approximately 21-24 days. Total lead time from approved artwork to UK delivery is typically 6-8 weeks. For US buyers, allow 7-9 weeks.

**Can corrugated toy packaging include die-cut windows?**

Yes. Die-cut windows can be open (no film) or fitted with a PET or PVC film insert. Open windows are fully recyclable. Plastic film inserts create mixed-material packaging that may affect recyclability claims and retailer requirements. Discuss your product display requirements and sustainability targets before specifying window construction.

**Is corrugated packaging suitable for heavy board games?**

Yes. For board games weighing 1-3kg, 3-ply B flute with full-overlap flap construction is the standard retail box specification. For master cartons packing 6-12 games, 5-ply BC double wall provides the compression strength needed for palletised storage and sea freight. Custom corrugated inserts are available for component organisation inside the main box.
