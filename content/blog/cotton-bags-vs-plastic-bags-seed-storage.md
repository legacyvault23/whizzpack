For most of the twentieth century, plastic bags were the dominant packaging for seed storage. They are cheap, waterproof, and easy to seal. Cotton bags fell out of favour as plastic became ubiquitous. In the last decade, that trend has reversed - not just for sustainability reasons, but because cotton's performance characteristics are genuinely better suited to seed storage than many seed producers and distributors realised.

This is a practical comparison. Both materials have real trade-offs, and the right choice depends on your seed type, storage conditions, and distribution needs.

## The Core Difference: Breathability

The most important distinction between cotton and plastic for seed storage is **breathability**.

Seeds are living organisms. Even in a dormant state, they respire - they exchange small amounts of gas with their environment. They also contain residual moisture from harvest and processing. In an airtight plastic bag, that moisture has nowhere to go. If the ambient temperature fluctuates - as it does in most farm storage conditions - condensation forms inside the bag. Condensation promotes mould and accelerates the breakdown of the seed coat, reducing germination rates.

Cotton fabric is permeable. It allows gentle air exchange, letting residual moisture dissipate gradually without allowing large-scale moisture ingress from outside. This makes cotton substantially better than standard polyethylene bags for seeds that will be stored for weeks or months before planting.

The caveat: cotton breathability is a double-edged property. In very humid storage conditions where external humidity is high, cotton bags can absorb that external humidity and transmit it to the seed. The ideal storage environment for cotton-bagged seeds is cool, dry, and well-ventilated.

---

## Germination Rate Comparison

The practical measure of whether packaging is working for seeds is germination rate. Several seed industry studies have compared storage outcomes for seeds in cotton vs plastic over equivalent periods:

- In controlled, dry storage (below 60% relative humidity), cotton and plastic perform comparably over periods up to three months.
- In variable storage conditions (humidity ranging from 50% to 75%), cotton bags consistently show 5 to 12 percentage points higher germination rates than sealed plastic bags over a six-month period. The mechanism is condensation prevention.
- For seeds stored more than six months, temperature-controlled sealed containers outperform both cotton and standard plastic bags. For very long-term storage, neither material is optimal without humidity control.

For commercial seed distributors whose product moves through the supply chain within a season, cotton bags are a clear upgrade over unsealed or loosely sealed plastic bags.

## Plastic Bags: Where They Still Win

Plastic is not without merit. There are specific contexts where it remains the better choice:

**Short-term moisture protection:** If seeds need to be protected from rain or high humidity during transport or at point of sale, plastic (particularly laminated woven polypropylene bags) provides a better moisture barrier than cotton.

**Very humid climates:** In tropical regions where outdoor humidity consistently exceeds 80%, cotton bags can absorb environmental moisture faster than seeds can tolerate. In these conditions, sealed, moisture-barrier packaging is safer.

**Low-cost, single-use applications:** For very high volume, low-value seed distributions where bags are used once and discarded, plain polyethylene is cheaper in absolute unit terms.

**Machine-fill compatibility:** Many high-speed automated seed-filling lines are optimised for plastic bags. Cotton bags can be machine-filled but require different handling and sealing equipment.

---

## Cotton Bags: Where They Win

**Breathable storage over 4 to 12 weeks:** Cotton is significantly better for seeds stored in non-climate-controlled farm or warehouse environments over a planting season.

**Premium and branded seed products:** Seed companies selling to conscious farmers or retailers increasingly use cotton bags as a quality signal. A seed product in a natural cotton bag communicates quality, craft, and care for the product in a way that a plastic bag does not.

**Retail and point-of-sale appeal:** For seed companies selling through garden centres, farm supply retailers, or online direct-to-consumer, cotton bags photograph better, feel better in the hand, and command higher perceived value. Our [guide to cotton seed bags for garden centres and seed companies](/blogs/cotton-seed-bags-garden-centers-seed-companies) covers the display specifications, certification requirements, and seasonal sourcing timelines that retail-facing seed brands need to plan for.

**Sustainability credentials:** Cotton bags are biodegradable, compostable, and reusable. Farmers who buy seed in cotton bags often reuse them for storage, labelling, or transplanting. This is a genuine supply chain sustainability benefit.

**GOTS-certified options:** For organic seed producers and distributors, GOTS-certified (Global Organic Textile Standard) cotton bags verify that the bag itself was produced without harmful chemical inputs - consistent with organic farming principles. Plastic bags have no equivalent certification.

**OEKO-TEX Standard 100:** For seed products that contact food, OEKO-TEX certified cotton fabric verifies the bag contains no harmful chemicals at contact levels. This matters for edible seed varieties.

## Practical Specifications: Cotton Bags for Seed Storage

Not all cotton bags are equivalent. The specification matters for seed storage:

**Fabric weight:** 150 to 200 GSM cotton canvas is standard for seed bags. Lighter fabrics (below 120 GSM) are too porous and provide less protection. Heavier fabrics (above 250 GSM) add weight and cost without meaningful benefit.

**Weave type:** Canvas or twill weave provides the right balance of breathability and protection. Loose weaves (like muslin or cheesecloth) are too permeable for most seed storage applications. Tight weaves (like duck canvas) reduce breathability without a commensurate protection benefit.

**Closure type:** Drawstring cotton bags provide secure, resealable closure without adhesive or heat sealing. The drawstring allows bags to be opened and resealed for sample inspection or partial use. For distribution and retail, sewn-top flat bags with an open hem are common.

**Size range:** Commercial seed bags range from 250g capacity (small vegetable seeds) to 25 kg capacity (bulk grain seed). Specify the fill weight and the seed type - a seed bag manufacturer will recommend dimensions based on bulk density.

**Printing:** Cotton bags can be flexographic-printed or screen-printed with brand name, variety name, lot number, and plant date. Printing directly on the bag eliminates the need for separate labels and reduces packaging components.

## The Sustainability Argument: Beyond Marketing

The environmental case for cotton over plastic in seed storage is not just a marketing angle. It is a genuine lifecycle difference:

**End of life:** A cotton seed bag that goes to landfill will biodegrade in 1 to 5 months. A plastic bag will persist for 200 to 500 years. For agricultural applications where bags end up in fields, this difference is significant. For operations that also use jute bags alongside cotton, our [jute vs cotton seed bags comparison](/blogs/jute-vs-cotton-seed-bags-comparison) covers how the two materials compare on breathability, certifications, and lifecycle sustainability.

**Reusability:** Cotton bags are routinely reused by farmers. A bag purchased for seed storage becomes a storage bag for harvested seed, a sample bag, a label bag, or even a cloth bag for other farm uses. Plastic bags rarely achieve this kind of extended use.

**Microplastic contamination:** Plastic bags in agricultural environments - broken down by UV, machinery, and soil microbes - generate microplastics that enter the soil. Cotton does not.

**Carbon:** Cotton production has its own carbon footprint, and GOTS certification addresses the chemical inputs in cotton growing. The lifecycle carbon comparison between cotton and plastic is complex and context-dependent. The end-of-life advantage for cotton is, however, unambiguous.

## Making the Choice

Cotton bags are the better choice for:
- Seed storage over 4 weeks in non-climate-controlled environments
- Premium and branded seed products
- Organic farming supply chains (GOTS-certified options available)
- Retail and direct-to-consumer seed sales
- Any context where sustainability credentials matter to the buyer

Plastic (particularly laminated woven PP) remains practical for:
- Short-term transport in high-humidity environments
- Very high-volume, low-value seed distributions where per-unit cost is the primary driver
- Automated filling lines not yet adapted for cotton

For most commercial seed distributors in the US and UK, a cotton bag specification in the 150 to 200 GSM drawstring or flat bag format will outperform standard plastic bags for in-season storage and will significantly improve brand perception among farmers who handle the product.
